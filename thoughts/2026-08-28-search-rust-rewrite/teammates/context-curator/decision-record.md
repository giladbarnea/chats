# Decision record

`state.md` carries what was decided. This carries **why the alternatives were rejected**, so a successor does not re-open a settled question with no way to know it was settled.

This mission exists because a completed rewrite finished and was never recorded durably. The mirror failure is one session-loss away: decisions surviving in `state.md` while the reasoning dies in message threads. This document is the counterweight.

**Provenance.** I was one half of most of these conversations. Where I hold a decision firsthand it is marked ◆. Where I know it only from what `search-firstmate` relayed to me, it is marked ○ and a successor should treat my account as a pointer rather than a source.

## ◆ 1. The branch is prior art, never an oracle

**Question.** `wip/cycle-02-native-default-pause-20260821` @ `0ffde41` holds a completed, independently verified native rewrite of all three journeys. Adopt it, or build?

**Ruling.** Prior art. Current `main`'s Python is the sole behavioural truth.

**Rejected: adopt it as the oracle.** Its expected outputs were generated by an implementation carrying known deviations. Validating against them validates against a known-imperfect mirror.

**Rejected: ignore it and build fresh.** Its 173 command shapes reproduce byte-for-byte against `main`'s Python, so the corpus is a sound legacy characterization and re-deriving it would waste days.

**What decided it.** Two measurements, from opposite directions. I replayed the 173 manifest cases against `main`'s Python: 166 exact, 7 differing only in the age-token colour, which is fixture rot in *their* harness rather than disagreement. `reviewer-profiler` independently ran the branch's own suite against a branch build and hit the identical seven.

**A successor must also know:** the branch's prose is unreliable and its code is not. Its own repair round overturned four of its nine closure-review findings. Read its source; treat its documents as leads.

**And:** adopting it wholesale reintroduces four defects `main` has already fixed — the fabricated broken-pipe traceback, the deleted escape validator, empty-string attribute parity, and terminal-width resolution. The reconciliation surface is two commits, **26 files, 913 insertions** — see decision 10 for why that number is not what I first said.

## ◆ 2. Neither reviewer converts to an implementer

**Question.** Packages need owners and both reviewers have capacity. Convert one?

**Ruling.** No. **Settled, not conditional** — see the resolution note below.

*Originally* the ruling carried a live condition: the offer stayed open, and if the roster question returned a definite no, `context-curator` would take a package and hand the review criteria to a named person in the same moment.

**That condition has expired.** The roster question was answered — two seats added, every package owned. The offer is no longer needed and the conditional is closed. A successor should read this entry as a settled decision, not as an open offer awaiting a trigger.

**Rejected: convert on capacity.** The value of the reviewer role did not come from knowing things — the knowledge is written down and transfers to anyone. It came from having no stake in any answer being right. Two of my own findings today were wrong and plausible; what made me audit them was having nothing invested. An implementer under handoff pressure has a reason to want their own numbers to hold.

**What decided it, and it is the argument a successor needs.** Converting a reviewer buys **at most one of three packages**. The trade is one package covered against losing the corpus-sweep and structural halves of G3 and G5 — and the structural half cannot be replaced, because it is the only review that reaches the four timing economies, which are byte-invisible and cost-unmeasured *by design*.

**Do not re-open this by observing that a reviewer is idle.** Idleness was priced in.

## ◆ 3. The oracle is pinned, and by route digest

**Question.** Everything rests on "`main`'s Python". What is that, exactly?

**Ruling, in three corrections.** First: the oracle is revision `8cb4c5f`. Then: a revision alone is not enough, because `.venv/bin/ch-legacy` reaches the *working tree* through the editable install — so every characterization records the revision **and** the tree state. Finally: a `git diff` digest cannot see the launcher or the installed `RECORD`, so a concurrent `uv sync` moves the oracle invisibly. The canonical recipe is `oracle_route_digest()` in `tests/oracle_digest.py`.

**Rejected: restamping artifacts with the new digest.** `contract-owner` caught this. A new digest on an artifact previously carrying a blind one asserts the oracle has not moved since generation — which the old stamp could never have supported. The restamp silently converts *unknown* into *verified*. Re-derive, or state the window unverified.

**What decided it.** The clock seam was the first oracle event and it caught the rule's own author: all eight of my findings still claimed a clean tree, three of them reached through the changed file. All eight held — but the belief that carried them, "they live in files nobody touched", was true and still not a proof.

**Also settled:** a stamp that does not apply dilutes the ones that do. Artifacts whose claims are about commits or tooling say `NOT ORACLE-DEPENDENT` explicitly.

## ◆ 4. Where the two behaviour sweeps stopped, and what was never covered

**Ruling.** Eight preserve-because-wrong behaviours, then stop. Five timing-shaped behaviours, then stop.

**What decided the first stop.** Yield collapsed with file size: five findings from ~200 lines of small utility helpers, three from ~3,500 lines of parser. The class lives where nobody thought hard, not in code where every branch is a deliberate rule.

**Never covered, recorded as a conscious gap rather than an oversight:** the three provider adapters' normalization rules, line by line — the bulk of `parsing.py`. Finding this class there needs differential execution against real payloads, not pattern reading. **If the port surfaces surprises in provider decoding, look there first and know that nobody looked.**

**What decided the second stop.** The method only finds *deliberate* economies, because those leave a test, a docstring, or a measurement. An accidental one leaves no trace. Closing that needs I/O counting against the oracle — not funded, also a conscious gap.

## ◆ 5. Gate two timing items, not five, and not one

**Ruling, revised the same day.** Originally: gate the per-ID flush only, treat the rest as documented properties, because their costs were unmeasured. Revised: gate the flush **and** early-close.

**What changed it.** I claimed no instrument could reach that class. `reviewer-profiler` built one and measured three of four. Early pager close **does not survive** on the branch build — it keeps scanning after the reader stops, so `| head` costs a full corpus scan for identical bytes. That gave it a measured cost and a demonstrated failure, which was the condition I had set for earning a gate.

**Rejected: gating all five.** Four slow tests guarding unmeasured costs is a net loss, and asserting a budget nobody measured is how the age fixture rotted.

**The correction that outlives the decision:** I wrote that the behaviours were unmeasurable. What I could support was that *our instruments at the time* could not see them. **A limit of your instruments is never a property of the world.** Had anyone believed me, that defect reaches G5.

## ◆ 6. Instrument conversion, and two freezes

**Ruling.** Before the oracle is deleted, every instrument consulting it must have its last consultation stored. This is a **prerequisite of the deletion slice**, not a follow-up — the work is cheap now and impossible afterwards.

**Amended after the fact — the clause I got wrong.** I framed the oracle as the perishable half. It is not the only one. `reviewer-profiler` found `branch_map_differential.py` had **never run**, because its driver lived in a session scratchpad and that session ended. So:

> **The driver is as perishable as the oracle, and it dies at any session exit — not at cutover.**

An instrument is not preserved by preserving the thing it consults. My own four happen to be on disk under `tests/data/` or my own directory, and both freezes are committed files — but that was habit, not design, and habit is not a guarantee.

**And the sharpening from `session-core`, which is the part that bites:** *preserving some of them is not preserving them.* They had copied drivers to the desk an hour before, and checked that **what they copied worked** rather than checking **which files the probes needed**. Their 355-case result was correct and unreproducible at the same time.

That is the aggregate rule again, one layer out: a green check on the subset you happened to copy says nothing about the subset you needed.

**The general form.** An instrument consulting two live routes dies at cutover; one consulting a route and a *stored* answer does not. Converting is re-posing the question so the second side is stored.

**The narrowing, from `contract-owner`.** Re-posing is free durability **only when the re-posed question is the same question**. Otherwise it buys durability by asking less.

**The test for it, and it is checkable without knowing what an instrument measures:** ask what the re-posed question answers if the new subject is wrong. **If the answer is still "pass", the question got weaker.**

**Worked example, because it is subtle.** My `nfc_nfd_probe` survives re-pointing perfectly and becomes worthless: it asks whether an implementation diverges on normalization form, and after cutover the native route *must* diverge to preserve behaviour — so it passes a route that diverges by nine characters or by ninety. Fixed by freezing the oracle's renders.

**Two freezes exist and cannot be recreated:**
- `tests/data/search-content-fuzz/frozen-oracle-age-colour/` — the oracle's bytes for the seven cases where it and the branch corpus disagree.
- `tests/data/search-content-fuzz/frozen-oracle-nfc-nfd/` — twelve renders that keep the probe asking the same question.

**The rule behind both, which did not exist when the first was done:** *a recorded disagreement must store both sides.* If a finding says X differs from Y and only Y is on disk, the finding dies when X becomes unavailable. This is the age-colour fixture rot seen from the other end — there the stored side was frozen and the live side moved; here the live side is about to vanish. Both are one-sided records of two-sided claims.

## ◆ 8. Method rules, and the mistakes that produced them

Each came from a near-miss, and **each near-miss produced a plausible number**. None was caught by the number looking wrong.

1. **No finding is reported from an aggregate alone** — dump and inspect the instances. Three saves. My own: a consistent off-by-one at four widths that was my width measure counting a zero-width space as one column. Perfectly plausible, entirely false.
2. **An invariant is only evidence over the modes where it is actually the contract.** Asserting a wrap rule against raw mode, whose contract is not to wrap, manufactures failures on correct behaviour.
3. **Instruments are imported, never copied.** A copy grades itself against a stale probe set and reports CALIBRATED while blind — demonstrated by building one.
4. **A limit of your instruments is never a property of the world.**
5. **A recorded disagreement must store both sides.**
6. **A discovery command that finds only the compliant cannot measure coverage.** `rg oracle_tree_state` returns the artifacts already stamped, never the ones at risk — I proposed that command and it was the wrong instrument for the question.
7. **A fixture whose expectation is deliberately wrong carries a comment saying so**, or the next reader repairs it in good faith and the divergence ships through our own contract.
8. **Never `text=True` on a capture.** Universal newlines rewrite carriage returns; a pty additionally applies ONLCR, so pty bytes may only be compared with pty bytes.

## ◆ 10. The reconciliation surface, and my own error

**Stated first as** "two commits, five files, 185 insertions". **Correct figure: 26 files, 913 insertions, 178 deletions.**

The first number was the `src/` and `rust/` slice only, because that is what I scoped the diff to, and three owners were sizing their port on it. The omitted 364 insertions are `tests/` — the guards that keep those fixes from regressing. An owner replaying source hunks alone restores the behaviours and none of the proofs.

**Recorded because the correction lived only in a message for hours** while two documents still carried the wrong number. A correction that lives only in a message thread has not been made.

## ○ 11. `search-firstmate`'s errors, in their own accounting

Recorded at their instruction. A successor who knows only the rulings will trust their own compressions.

- **Five relay compressions.** Two I saw firsthand: "Python does not flush" passed on as a general rule when it was true at one of three sites; and my port-sizing figure circulated after correction. Both travelled because a finding was compressed in the telling.
- **A homegrown digest recipe** published when a better one existed in the tree — and then advice to restamp, as though a stamp were self-certifying. The second would have left a false assurance in eight documents.
- **An age-band instruction** that would have rebuilt the fixture rot the team had just diagnosed.

Their own conclusion, which I would keep: **a finding must state its scope explicitly rather than leaving it inferable.**

## ○ 12. Three decisions I hold only secondhand

I was not party to these. A successor should get them from `state.md` and `teammates/search-firstmate/RESUME.md` rather than from me.

- **The launcher window is exclusive, and announcing on *grant* matters as much as on release.** A failing build leaves no binary at all, because the installer deletes before it replaces — so a red tree blocks every install for everyone.
- **PyO3 bindings are not widened for testability**, while the clock injection *was* justified. I hold the outcome, not the line between them.
- **Instrument conversion is a prerequisite of the deletion slice**, not a follow-up. I hold the reasoning for this one (decision 6); I do not hold how it was sequenced against the other slices.
